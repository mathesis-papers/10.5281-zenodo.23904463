Refuting the X-MEMS closest-separable-state conjecture in arXiv:2209.01430v2, Appendix A, Eq. (A11)

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

When measuring how far a quantum state is from having no entanglement, can one safely search only a conveniently patterned family of candidates? Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang answer no for a conjecture of Consiglio, Apollaro and Wieśniak: a three-qubit example has an explicitly unentangled approximation closer than every candidate of the proposed form.

A qubit is a two-level quantum system. A joint state's density matrix records outcome probabilities on its diagonal and quantum coherences off the diagonal. Independently preparing each qubit and randomly mixing such preparations produces a fully separable state. The paper's distance is the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm: the sum of squared absolute differences between corresponding matrix entries. Minimizing this over fully separable states geometrically measures departure from full separability. A justified restriction on the minimizing state's shape would simplify this difficult search.

The conjecture in the earlier authors' 2022 work restricts both coherences and diagonal probabilities. Ma and Zhang examine its three-qubit low-branch instance. Their target gives probability one fifth to each of 000, 001, 010, 011 and 111, zero elsewhere, and coherence 11/112 between 000 and 111. Proposed approximations have equal endpoint probabilities, equal probabilities within 001, 010, 011, and equal probabilities within 100, 101, 110. Do those restrictions still permit a genuinely closest fully separable state?

The authors establish an exact restricted minimum of 109/23520. Positivity bounds each single-qubit coherence by diagonal probabilities; arithmetic–geometric mean inequalities turn this into a linear constraint preserved under mixtures. Completing squares then bounds every restricted candidate's distance. Attainment is explicit: average nine product states over correlated phases, taking the local probabilities of zero to be 9/10, 1/2, 1/2, then mix this average with the state 111 using weights 5/6 and 1/6. Changing the last two local probabilities to 199/400 produces another mixture of ten product states with strictly smaller distance. This refutes the conjecture without finding the unrestricted optimum.

The better competitor still has only the two corner entries off the diagonal: it breaks population equalities, not sparsity. Symmetry alone cannot justify those equalities. Swapping just 000 and 111 fixes the target but turns a product superposition of 000 and 001 into an entangled superposition of 111 and 001, leaving the admissible set. Correlated local phases and permutations of all but the first qubit do preserve full separability. Averaging over these genuine symmetries justifies sparse support and corresponding permutation invariance, but not all the conjectured equalities.

Related examples distinguish notions linked in the earlier discussion. One three-qubit state is separable across every one-versus-two-qubit division and passes partial transposition, a necessary matrix-positivity test, yet is not fully separable: different divisions can require incompatible decompositions. Two-qubit examples show that concurrence, another entanglement indicator, neither determines nor consistently orders this squared distance. Equal concurrence can accompany unequal distances; larger concurrence can accompany smaller distance. The authors also give analogous multipartite examples. These counterexamples do not invalidate the earlier work's separate two-qubit distance formulas.

A positive result survives independently. For any n-qubit state whose only off-diagonal entries are opposite corners, suppose their coherence magnitude approaches g as n grows. The authors prove that the squared distance approaches 2g². Removing these entries leaves a fully separable diagonal state, giving an upper bound. Every fully separable state's corner magnitude is at most 2⁻ⁿ, because each local coherence is at most one half. The unavoidable corner mismatch supplies a lower bound converging to the same value. Arbitrary diagonal probabilities are allowed.

The manuscript supplies written arguments for these results. Its reported finite computation checks selected exact identities and constants, not all separable states. The described Lean formalization covers the counterexample to the universal shape assertion; attainment of the restricted minimum, symmetry reduction and later consequences are supported here by written proofs, not included in that claimed formal coverage. The actual unrestricted minimum for the exhibited target and a general finite-qubit description of closest states remain open. The contribution is a precise failure of an optimization shortcut while preserving the large-system limit; the earlier general algorithm and its performance are outside this note's scope.
